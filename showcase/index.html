3 · How it works

Four things the pipeline does, then a person signs

Data in

Raw sources

Monthly reporting export, business notes, HR and operations. Unclean, from several systems.

Step · 1

Clean & structure

Fixes dates, names, duplicates and text amounts. Flags anything that looks like an entry error.

Steps · 2 to 4

Analyse

Variance vs budget with materiality tests, rolling forecast, one-pagers, and a check that no raw data leaves the company.

Analyst

FP&A analyst

Explains the gaps the data can't, reviews the draft commentary and signs off.

Results

Reports out

Variance analysis, forecast, executive summary, and a one-pager for each business line.

Automated step, same result every run Human input, where finance judgment is needed

The system flags 20 material variances and explains only the 4 backed by a dated business note. The other 16 go to the analyst: 14 now carry a written explanation, labelled as analyst input, and 2 stay open. In this demo those 14 explanations are written for illustration, since the variances behind them are generated noise. What the pack guarantees is that it never mixes the three kinds: documented evidence, analyst input and open items are labelled on every line.

4 · What stays with the analyst

The pipeline does the repeatable work. The analyst explains and signs.

The cleaning, the materiality tests and the first draft run the same way every month. The analyst explains what the data can't and signs off.

The mechanical work takes minutes

Cleaning, variance tests, forecast refresh and one-pagers run identically every month. The analyst's time goes to the open questions.

Commentary you can defend

Every cause in the pack is either documented, labelled as analyst input, or marked openly unexplained. In a board meeting, that difference matters.

No invented numbers

The language model only rewords analysis that is already computed and checked. Every figure in its text is traced back to a source, and an untraceable one fails the run.

The data stays in-house

Only one step talks to an AI service, and only with aggregated summaries. The monthly export never leaves the machine, and a check confirms it every run.

A concrete example: one line in the export read 52,243,584 in a business line whose normal month is EUR5 million, with costs and payroll unchanged. A rushed month turns that into a growth story. Here it was flagged as a probable typo, kept out of every number and every sentence, and sent to the analyst for correction. The operations data agreed: a normal month, 17 projects delivered against 17 planned.
